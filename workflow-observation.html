Watch the decision before timing the task

For a week, observe consecutive cases across ordinary and busy periods. Ask the worker to walk through one routine case and one that needed help. Count a case once even when it changes hands. Add up each person's active minutes and keep a separate column for time spent waiting. A file can sit overnight and still need only two minutes of work.

What the six rows justify

In this sample, 50% of cases qualify for draft reminders. Those cases account for one-third of the staff time. To reproduce the estimate, use the rows above:

  1. Add every case’s active minutes to get 36 staff minutes.
  2. Add the minutes for cases A, B and D to get 12 minutes eligible for removal. Divide 12 by 36 to get one-third.
  3. If drafting removes all 12 eligible minutes, subtract one new review minute for each of the three drafts. The estimate is 9 staff minutes saved for this batch.

Using half of all minutes as the removable share would estimate 18 minutes before review. That overstates the eligible effort by six minutes. A timed pilot will show how much of the eligible work can actually be removed.

Which records can measure active staff time?

If your system logs the start and finish of each action, check whether those timestamps cover work the person was actively doing. When your log records only status changes, observe or time the step yourself. Microsoft’s process mining metric definitions likewise use event duration to calculate active time.
