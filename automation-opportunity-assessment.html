What to record before choosing a pilot

Task and owner
Who owns the process? What event starts the task, and what output ends it?
Baseline
How many items arrive each week? How many active staff minutes does each item take? Record waiting time separately.
Rule and exceptions
What information is available when the decision happens? Which cases can follow one documented rule? Who reviews the rest?
Recoverable time
Estimate the minutes of original work the automation would remove. Subtract any new time spent checking the automation or fixing its output.
Quality and risk
Which mistake would matter most? How often does that mistake happen today? How will you detect it and reverse it?
Pilot decision
Define the batch, a measurable success threshold, and the condition that stops the pilot. Name the person who decides.

Worked example with hypothetical invoice exceptions

A team handles 30 invoice exceptions per week at 12 staff minutes per case. It estimates that a routing rule could remove 75% of that work. The baseline is 360 minutes. The rule could remove 270 minutes, with 120 minutes needed for new review. That leaves 150 minutes of staff time recovered per week.

For the pilot, the team compares a week of suggested routes against the normal review. It advances if staff would recover at least 60 minutes without sending an invoice to the wrong reviewer. A wrong route triggers a review of the rule. Cases with missing or conflicting supplier information go to a human. Payment approval stays with the existing approver.
